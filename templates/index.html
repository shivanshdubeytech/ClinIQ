Emergency SOS & First-Aid Guide

Immediate action protocols for acute medical emergencies.

In a life-threatening emergency, call for help first! Do not delay professional medical dispatch. ClinIQ is an educational reference.
112 Global / EU / IN 911 US / CAN 999 UK Poison 1-800-222-1222 988 Crisis / 988

Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (Hands-Only CPR)

Unresponsive & Not Breathing Normally
Call 112 / 911
  1. Check Response: Tap shoulders firmly and shout loudly: "Are you okay?" Check chest for normal breathing (occasional gasping is NOT breathing).
  2. Call 911 / 112 & Get an AED: Put your phone on speaker. Shout for a bystander to bring an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) immediately.
  3. Hand Position: Place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest (lower breastbone). Interlock fingers of your other hand on top. Lock elbows straight.
  4. Push Hard & Fast: Compress at 100–120 beats per minute (to the rhythm of the song "Stayin' Alive"). Push at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep. Allow chest to fully recoil after each push.
  5. Continuous Compressions: Do not interrupt compressions until EMS personnel arrive or an AED is powered on and prompting you.
NEVER stop compressions to check for a pulse if untrained. Continuous hands-only compressions maintain vital blood flow to the brain and heart.

Stroke Emergency — The F.A.S.T. Assessment

Time Is Brain Tissue
Call 112 / 911
F Face Drooping

Ask them to smile. Does one side of the face droop or look uneven?

A Arm Weakness

Ask them to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward or feel numb?

S Speech Difficulty

Ask them to repeat: "The sky is blue." Is words slurred, garbled, or hard to understand?

T Time to Call 911 / 112

If they show ANY of these signs, call emergency immediately. Note the exact time symptoms began!

DO NOT give food, drinks, or aspirin! If the stroke is hemorrhagic (bleeding in brain), aspirin or blood thinners can worsen bleeding.

Choking Relief — Heimlich Maneuver

Airway Obstruction
Call 112 / 911
  1. Assess Airway: Ask: "Are you choking?" If they can speak, cough forcefully, or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing. Do NOT interfere.
  2. 5 Firm Back Blows: If unable to breathe, cough, or speak (silent coughing, hands clutching throat), lean them forward. Deliver 5 firm blows between shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  3. 5 Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich): Stand behind the person. Wrap arms around their waist. Make a fist with thumb side against their abdomen, just above the navel. Grasp fist with your other hand.
  4. Thrust Inward & Upward: Pull sharply inward and upward 5 times as if lifting the person off their feet.
  5. Repeat 5 and 5: Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the blockage pops out or person can breathe.
  6. If person goes unconscious: Lower them to the ground, call emergency immediately, and begin CPR compressions.

Severe Bleeding & Hemorrhage Control

Rapid Blood Loss
Call 112 / 911
  1. Call emergency dispatch if bleeding is pulsing, spurting, pooling, or does not slow after pressure.
  2. Direct Firm Pressure: Cover the wound with a clean cloth, sterile gauze, or clothing. Press down firmly with both hands using your body weight.
  3. Maintain Continuous Pressure: Do NOT lift the cloth to peek. If blood soaks through, add more layers on top and continue pressing hard.
  4. Elevate: If on an arm or leg and no broken bones are suspected, elevate the injured limb above the heart.
  5. Tourniquet (Severe limb trauma): If spurting blood from an arm or leg won't stop, apply a commercial tourniquet 2–3 inches above the wound (never over a joint). Tighten until bleeding stops. Record the exact time applied.
NEVER remove impaled objects (e.g. knives, glass shards). Stabilize the object in place with rolled cloths and wait for emergency surgeons.

Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

Chest Tightness / Radiating Pain
Call 112 / 911
  1. Recognize Symptoms: Crushing chest tightness or squeezing; pain radiating to left shoulder, arm, neck, or jaw; shortness of breath, clammy cold sweat, nausea.
  2. Call 911 / 112 immediately: Every minute saved protects heart muscle. Do not attempt to drive yourself.
  3. Rest in W-Position: Have the person sit on the floor, leaning back against a wall with knees bent and supported. Loosen tight collars, ties, and belts.
  4. Chew Aspirin (if eligible): If not allergic, not told to avoid aspirin by a doctor, and no active bleeding, offer 1 adult aspirin (325 mg) or 2–4 low-dose baby aspirins (chewed, not swallowed whole).
  5. Be CPR-Ready: If the person loses consciousness and stops breathing normally, begin CPR immediately.

Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)

Airway Swelling / Epinephrine
Call 112 / 911
  1. Warning Signs: Swelling of throat, lips, or tongue; difficulty breathing, audible wheezing; widespread hives, dizziness, vomiting, rapid weak pulse.
  2. Inject Epinephrine (EpiPen): Remove safety cap. Push the orange/needle tip firmly into the outer middle thigh (can go directly through pants). Hold firmly for 3 seconds. Massage area for 10 seconds.
  3. Call Emergency Immediately: Always call 911 / 112 even if symptoms appear to improve—a secondary rebound reaction can occur within hours.
  4. Positioning: Lay person flat with legs elevated. If breathing is difficult or they are vomiting, allow them to sit upright.
  5. Second Dose: If symptoms do not improve after 5 to 15 minutes and paramedics have not arrived, administer a second auto-injector in the other leg.

Seizures & Convulsions

Protect Airway & Head
Call 112 / 911
  1. Cushion the head: Place a folded jacket, pillow, or hands under their head. Clear away nearby hard, sharp, or dangerous objects.
  2. Time the Seizure: Note the start time. Call 911 / 112 if seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, if a second seizure begins immediately, or if the person is injured or pregnant.
  3. Recovery Position: Once active jerking stops, roll the person gently onto their side into the recovery position so saliva/fluids drain from mouth.
  4. Stay Calm & Reassure: Stay with the person until they are fully awake and alert.
NEVER hold them down or put ANYTHING in their mouth! It is impossible to swallow the tongue, and objects can shatter teeth or completely choke the airway.

Severe Burns & Scalds

Cool & Protect
Call 112 / 911
  1. Cool with running water: Hold the burned skin under cool running tap water for 10 to 20 minutes. Do NOT use ice, freezing water, or butter/toothpaste (causes severe tissue damage and infection).
  2. Remove jewelry/tight items: Gently remove rings, bracelets, or loose clothing before rapid swelling begins. Do NOT peel away clothing stuck to the burn.
  3. Cover loosely: Cover the burn with a clean, sterile non-adherent dressing or clean plastic food wrap.
  4. Call 911 / 112 if: Burn is larger than the person's hand, covers the face, hands, feet, or groin, or is caused by electricity or chemicals.
DO NOT pop blisters! Intact blister skin is a natural sterile barrier against life-threatening bacterial infections.
